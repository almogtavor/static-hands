Installation

Let's discover Static Hands in less than 5 minutes.

Pick your operating system. The tab for the computer you are on right now opens automatically.

  • Windows
  • macOS

Download Static Hands for Windows#

  • Option one: Click the following button and install the "install.exe" file.

Click to Immediately Install !

caution

Notice that the exe has been compiled without certificates. Anti-Virus softwares might warn about it and you should ignore. If you prefer not to install an exe, you can go with option two.

  • Option two: Download AutoHotKey. Clone project

    $ git clone https://github.com/almogtavor/static-hands.git

    go to static-hands --> Windows --> run_v2.ahk with AutoHotkey.

    $ cd static-hands\windows$ run_v2.ahk

    To put ahk into effect automatically every time PC start:

    • Select run_v2.ahk, and press Ctrl+C.

    • Press Win+R to open the Run dialog, then enter shell:startup and click OK or Enter.

      This will open the Startup folder for the current user.

    • Right-click inside the window, and click "Paste Shortcut". The shortcut to the script should now be in the Startup folder.

Install Static Hands on macOS#

One line in the Terminal app installs everything.

1. Paste one line into Terminal#

Open the Terminal app (press ⌘ Space, type Terminal, press Enter). Copy this line, paste it into the Terminal window and press Enter:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/almogtavor/static-hands/main/mac/install.sh | bash

The installer does everything it can for you: it installs Karabiner-Elements (the free app that remaps the keys) if you don't have it, adds the Static Hands rules, and switches them on. What is left are a few clicks that macOS only accepts from you.

2. Let the installer run#

If Karabiner-Elements is not on your Mac yet, its own installer appears: click Continue, then Install, and enter your password.

3. Allow Karabiner-Elements#

Any app that changes what your keys do needs your permission, and nothing works until you give it. macOS does not always ask on its own, so open System Settings (Apple menu → System Settings) and turn on these three switches. You only do this once.

  1. Background services. Go to General → Login Items & Extensions. Under Allow in the Background, turn on every Karabiner-Elements entry. Some of them are listed under the developer's name, Fumihiko Takayama. Turn those on too.
  2. Driver extension. On the same page, scroll down to Extensions, click the ⓘ next to Driver Extensions, turn on Karabiner-DriverKit-VirtualHIDDevice and enter your Mac password.
  3. Input Monitoring. Go to Privacy & Security → Input Monitoring and turn on Karabiner-Core-Service. If it is not in the list yet, do steps 1 and 2 first and look again.

If macOS shows a pop-up asking to allow Karabiner-Elements under Accessibility, allow that as well.

Still nothing when you hold CapsLock?

Go through the three switches again; one that is still off is almost always the reason. Then quit Karabiner-Elements from its menu bar icon and open it again, or restart your Mac.

The picture is a simplified drawing and the names are from macOS 15 (Sequoia). On macOS 13 and 14 the first switch is under General → Login Items, and the driver extension is approved with an Allow button in Privacy & Security.

4. Try it#

Click into any text field, hold CapsLock and press I, J, K or L.

Differences on a Mac
  • CapsLock + U / O send the Mac's Home / End keys, which many Mac apps treat as "top / bottom of the document".
  • The left-hand modifiers are F = Option, D = Shift, S = Control, W = Command.
  • Pressing CapsLock on its own no longer toggles capital letters.

Turning it off#

Open Karabiner-Elements → Complex Modifications and click the trash icon next to Static Hands Basic Configuration. Your previous Karabiner settings are also backed up in ~/.config/karabiner/backups every time the installer runs.
